# KATO N scale library: sources and geometry

Research checked on 7 October 2026. The library contains **143 placeable entries**: 17 single-track straights, 19 single-track curves, 5 turnout/crossover entries, 3 diamond crossings, 16 double-track or slab pieces, 15 viaduct pieces, 29 bridge color/shape variants, and 39 scenery accessories. The physical-planning audit retains 91 entries with verified principal catalog dimensions and marks 52 entries nominal where a footprint, support datum, connector template, or banking transition remains uncertain.

Every entry identifies its KATO product code and links to the product page or the exact catalog page used. These are individual placeable pieces or scenery models, rather than complete retail packs. For example, both a 29 mm and a 45.5 mm adjustment straight belong to assortment **20-091**; selecting either entry places one piece. Retail stock and pack quantities are not tracked.

During the initial network restriction, a [public mirror of the track PDF](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hrefcl/kato-unitrack/main/data/source/us_unitrack_1-40_20251028.pdf) was useful for research. After access became available, the PDF was downloaded directly from KATO USA. Its SHA-256 hash matched the mirrored document exactly. The mirror repository's AI-extracted JSON was used to locate candidate pages only; catalog identity and dimensions were checked against the actual PDF and official product pages.

## Measurements and simplified models

Track units are millimeters. Curve angles are radians in code, and `length` is the centerline arc length: radius × angle. Double-track spacing is 33 mm. For double curves, `innerRadius` and `outerRadius` are the actual catalog rail-route radii; the stored `radius` is their midpoint. In particular, KATO's large double curve is **480/447 mm**, not 481/448 mm. Single curved deck-girder bridges are the distinct **R448-15°** and **R481-15°** products.

The original save identifiers `s248`, `s124`, `s62`, `c249`, `c282`, `c315`, and `c348` are preserved. **R348-45°** is SKU **20-132**; **20-130** is the separate R348-30° piece. The compact curve entries include true catalog products down to R117. Tight compact curves are designed for selected short vehicles; their availability in the library does not imply that a long E235 train is physically compatible with them.

`verification: 'verified'` means the product code and main track centerline measurements were checked against an official source. It does not claim that the procedural mesh is a scan of the product, that an assembled layout passes all physical checks, or that a manufacturer certifies this app. Real sleeper spacing, joiner mechanisms, rail frogs, molding details, and superelevation are simplified for browser performance.

`verification: 'nominal'` applies to all accessory meshes, the adjustable straight at its default 93 mm midpoint, switches/crossovers, 15° diamond crossings, and the banking approach pieces. Their real product identities remain sourced, but some rendered dimensions or routes are approximations:

- The expansion track's real adjustment range is 78–108 mm. The simulator initially places it at 93 mm.
- The #4 and #6 turnout catalog branch radii are 481 mm and 718 mm. Their simulated branches use idealized 15° arcs; the lead-in, frog, and branch endpoints are not exact engineering templates. The double crossover has its real 310 mm overall length with nominal interior routes.
- A 15° diamond crossing has its catalog 186 mm main route and crossing angle, but its four connector positions and secondary-route length are not established by an exact verified template. A 90° crossing has both routes dimensioned at 124 mm in the catalog diagram.
- Double-curve approaches have verified radii and 22.5° plan angles, but the current generic entry does not distinguish the actual pack's two different flat-to-banked/banked-to-flat pieces. They must remain subject to physical compatibility review.
- A platform's catalog length can be exact while its rendered width, canopy, signage, and other scenery dimensions remain nominal. The viaduct station's 248 × 113 mm footprint is sourced, while its height is approximate.
- A signal accessory represents the signal scenery from **20-606-1**, whose physical product includes a 124 mm straight. The buffer represents the stop from **20-046**, whose physical product includes a 62 mm straight. The crossing-gate accessory represents scenery from **20-027-1**, whose physical product includes a 124 mm rerailer. Placing these scenery entries does not add their supplied rails automatically.
- The bridge color variants use verified product codes, lengths, and catalog color names, with approximate display colors.

The library intentionally focuses on individually useful track, bridges, stations, and scenery. Electrical controllers, cables, packaged layout sets, HO products, UNITRAM street plates, flexible track, turntables, special widening sections, and unimplemented wye/compact point geometries are not placeable entries in this version. This is a broad sourced library, not a claim of the entire KATO product range.

## Audit for building a physical layout

The following distinctions prevent a visually connected layout from being presented as a verified shopping plan.

| Part or condition | Confirmed source data | Remaining physical-planning limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| #4 turnout, 20-220/20-221 | 481 mm nominal branch radius, 15° branch, 126 mm main route. The 126 mm length follows the annotated official 248 mm assembly: turnout + S60 + S62. | Exact branch port and lead-in geometry are not supplied as a measured manufacturing template. The special auxiliary pieces supplied with the turnout are not generic S62 pieces. |
| #6 turnout, 20-202/20-203 | 718 mm nominal branch radius, 15° branch, 186 mm main route. The official #6 + R718 configuration has 49.5 mm centers; adding S64, as in plan 02-1A, gives 66 mm centers. | A pure R718 × 15° arc produces a lateral offset of 24.465 mm, so two arcs produce 48.930 mm. Adding an ideal 64 mm diagonal gives about 65.494 mm. Nominal radius/angle must not be promoted to exact connector geometry. |
| Double crossover, 20-210 | 310 mm overall main length and 33 mm lane spacing. | Interior crossover paths, frog positions, and rolling-stock clearances are nominal. |
| Double-track banked curves | Radius pairs 315/282, 414/381, and 480/447 mm, each at 45°; corresponding approaches are 22.5°. | A flat straight must connect through an appropriate approach; opposite banking directions cannot be judged from coincident plan-view endpoints alone. |
| Viaduct banked curves, 20-544 | 414/381 mm at 45°, with 20-545 approach pieces at 22.5°. | KATO explicitly says direct connection to a straight can cause derailment, and requires the approach track. The flat side faces the straight; the banked side faces the banked curve. |
| Standard pier component heights | 23-017, 23-019, and 23-047 are cataloged as 50 mm components. | Component height alone does not establish the roadbed-bottom support height. The app does not automatically certify an arbitrary elevated placement as supported by these parts. |
| Tapered pier, 23-069 | Explicitly 50 mm pier height and 60 mm supported track/roadbed-bottom height. | Its lateral footprint and detailed attachment mesh remain nominal; support contact and footprint clearances still need checking. |
| Raising kit, 23-068 + 23-069 | 90 or 110 mm pier height, giving 100 or 120 mm roadbed-bottom height respectively. | This is a combination of two purchased products. It is not a single taller SKU, and the current library does not substitute an elevated or stretched ordinary pier for the assembly. |
| Graded pier sets | Official basic-set packaging says “Incline Pier Set 15–50 mm.” Basic set 23-015-1 contains No.1–5; auxiliary 23-016-1 contains No.0.5, 1.5, 2.5, 3.5, and 4.5. | An individually dimensioned table and support datum for every level were not confirmed. Do not infer 10 mm increments, intermediate support heights, or a universal safe gradient from the product numbers. |

`KATO_SUPPORT_DATA` exports the distinction between `pierHeight` and `trackElevation`. Only an explicitly verified `trackElevation` is a usable roadbed-bottom datum for an automatically proposed support. `supportComponentHeight: 50` and `supportDeckHeight: 60` on the tapered-pier entry document the same distinction. Its rendered height of 60 mm represents the support assembly, including the 10 mm attachment/viaduct structure; it does not claim the pier column itself is 60 mm tall. Positions above table level do not create another purchaseable KATO support height.

The current E235 product page documents an R315 starter layout for smooth running. `E235_ENGINEERING_DATA.recommendedRadiusMm` therefore uses 315 mm as the conservative planning recommendation. Its `minimumRadiusMm` remains unset: the inspected E235 source does not publish that number. KATO USA's separate R249 general design reference is explicitly identified by `generalDesignRadiusMm` and `generalDesignSourceUrl`. No E235-specific maximum grade or guaranteed train length on a grade was established from the inspected official sources. An app planning target such as 3% is a chosen design target, not a KATO traction specification. Additional cars, curves, and abrupt changes of slope affect real operation.

Same-scale UNITRACK rail sections use UniJoiners, but rail connection alone does not establish compatibility of viaduct walls, S-Joiner structures, banking, track centers, or vehicle clearances. The official catalog notes that a truss bridge adjacent to some curved viaducts may require a short S62V or S124V straight to avoid contact. General double-track spacing is 33 mm; the [official #6 configurations](https://unitrack.katomodels.com/products/line_single/branch_cross) use 49.5 mm without S64 and 66 mm with S64, so 33 mm must not be treated as the spacing of every possible yard configuration. The R348-45° single viaduct also requires an intermediate pier/support according to the official elevated-track page.

The seven incline step, numbered pier, and SPC entries used by [plan 02-1A](kato-plan02-1a.md) have nominal roadbed datums and remain unverified in `KATO_SUPPORT_DATA`. Their fixed modeled heights do not add measured manufactured sizes or make them eligible for the ramp builder's verified-support selection. The ordinary No.5 column's sourced 50 mm height does not independently verify its modeled 60 mm roadbed datum.

The public [KATO USA Track Builder](https://katousa.com/track-builder/) was inspected as a possible connector-data source. Its page data is not suitable as a verified engineering template: WX310 is given a 310 mm length but only 248 mm between its stored end coordinates; #4 connector coordinates span 124 mm despite the official standard assembly implying 126 mm; and several 22.5° approach items reuse a 45° graphic and angle. These conflicts are recorded rather than silently replacing catalog dimensions with the builder's coordinates. No geometry is described as certified solely because it appears in an official web tool.

Purchase planning should retain unresolved items as explicit review requirements. Verified dimensions, an in-app connected loop, or a drawn support are each useful evidence, but none alone establishes a complete, rigid, collision-free, powered physical railway.

## Appearance reference

Actual product photographs in the official PDF were inspected for the 3D materials and construction. Standard single track has a beveled, textured gray ballast roadbed, dark wooden sleepers, nickel-silver rails, and visible joiner ends. Concrete-tie pieces use pale sleepers. Single-track viaduct sections have darker raised retaining walls. Modern double-track viaducts use pale concrete walls and slab track. Truss bridges have open steel frames; plate-girder and deck-girder bridges have distinct solid or underneath supports. The catalog photographs on pages 10, 16, and 21 were the main visual references.

An [official KATO E235 Yamanote diorama photograph](https://s3-ap-northeast-1.amazonaws.com/kato-model/product/images/2024073114040766a9c5c784fa3/E235yamanote_nmi_m_1.jpg), embedded on the [current E235 product page](https://www.katomodels.com/product/n/e235_yamanote_slm), was also downloaded and inspected. It shows a silver E235 with lime-green vertical door panels and a lime front surrounding dark cab glazing; the lower cab face uses a dotted fade. The elevated tracks in this reference use pale concrete sleepers, mottled gray ballast, silver rails, pale viaduct parapets, and separate steel girder structures. The public image host requires the normal product-page HTTP referrer when downloading the embedded photograph.

The app uses original procedural geometry and materials. The manufacturers' product photographs and PDF are linked as research sources and are not bundled as application textures or presented as app-owned artwork.

The #4/#6 turnouts show two tapered moving point blades, fixed heel pivots, and a connecting bar; the double crossover shows four such pairs. Static switch rails are omitted in the blade zones so the moving rails visibly open and close against the stock rails. The modeled 1.75 mm throw and 360 ms travel time are visual choices, not measured KATO mechanism specifications. Switch movement does not change the catalog endpoints or route centerlines. Opening a saved railway restores its point positions directly; switch controls animate from their current positions, including when reversed during a throw.
